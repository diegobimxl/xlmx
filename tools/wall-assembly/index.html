Developer ROI Framework

ICF vs. standard wood frame: $8-15/SF installed premium → 20-30% heating/cooling reduction → 6-10 yr payback in zone 5+. Mass timber (CLT) vs. concrete: similar structural cost at scale but 40-60% lower embodied carbon A1-A3 → carbon credit value $8-25/tCO2e. Double stud / Passive House assemblies add $12-20/SF but can eliminate mechanical heating in zones 5-6 (saves $8-18/SF in HVAC first cost).

BIM / Drafting Note

In Revit Wall Types: build compound layers matching the assembly. Assign Thermal Resistance (R) to each layer. Use the Energy Settings to toggle between clear-field and framing R-values — always export with the "Framing Fraction" parameter set to match the actual stud spacing (typically 15% for 16"OC, 12% for 24"OC). For thermal bridging compliance per ASHRAE 90.1 App. A, use the U-factor path to account for linear transmittance (ψ) at interfaces.

Code Reference

ASHRAE 90.1-2022 Table 5.5: use the effective R-value (framing included) for compliance, not clear-field. IBC 2021 Chapter 7: fire-resistance requirements by occupancy and construction type. IECC 2021 Table R402.1.2: residential minimum R-values by climate zone. NFPA 285 test required for foam plastic exterior insulation (CI) on combustible wall assemblies. Mass timber (CLT) fire ratings per AWC CLT Handbook and ASTM E119 tests (typically 2-hr for 5-layer CLT).

Effective R-values based on ASHRAE Fundamentals Handbook isothermal planes method. Framing fraction: 15% for 16"OC, 12% for 24"OC. Costs are rough order-of-magnitude installed, excluding finishes. Embodied carbon (EC) A1-A3 from EC3 / NIST BEES industry averages. Verify with local contractor pricing and current material EPDs.
