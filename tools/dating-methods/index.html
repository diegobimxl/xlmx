Dendrochronology (Tree-Ring Dating)

Each tree ring represents one year of growth. Ring width and density vary with climate. By matching overlapping sequences from living trees, historical timber, and sub-fossil wood, a master chronology is built.

MAXIMUM CHRONOLOGY EXTENT
~14,000
years (Hohenheim oak/pine chronology)
Most useful for dating wooden structures, ships, panel paintings, and waterlogged organic remains. Also used to calibrate ¹⁴C curves. Cannot directly date non-wood materials. Error: ±1 year when ring counted from felling date.

Thermoluminescence (TL) & Optically Stimulated Luminescence (OSL)

Ionizing radiation from natural radioactivity displaces electrons into traps in mineral crystals. When heated (TL) or exposed to light (OSL), trapped electrons are released as light. The intensity reveals accumulated dose since last reset event.

EFFECTIVE DATE RANGE
10 ka – 1 Ma
kiloyears to megayears
TL: resets at firing (ceramics, burnt flint). OSL: resets at sun exposure (sediment burial). Typical error: ±5–10%. Requires measurement of environmental dose rate. Used for sediment dating, fired ceramics, brick, and tile.
t = (1/λ) × ln(1 + ⁴⁰Ar/⁴⁰K) — λ = 5.543×10⁻¹⁰ yr⁻¹

Half-life of ⁴⁰K = 1.25 Ga. Effective range: ~100,000 yr to 4.6 Ga. Requires volcanic material (lava, ash, pumice). Assumes all ⁴⁰Ar at time of formation was zero (complete outgassing). Used extensively for dating early hominid sites in East Africa.
