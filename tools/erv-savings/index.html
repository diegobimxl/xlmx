ERV vs HRV

HRV (Heat Recovery) transfers sensible heat only. ERV also transfers moisture (latent heat). In hot/humid climates, ERV outperforms HRV by pre-conditioning moisture. In cold dry climates, HRV may be preferred to avoid excess humidification. Rotary wheel ERVs have highest total effectiveness (80%+ sensible + 70%+ latent).

ASHRAE 90.1 Requirement

ASHRAE 90.1-2022 §6.5.6.1: ERV required when design ventilation rate exceeds 3,000 CFM AND the difference between outdoor and indoor temperature/humidity exceeds ASHRAE limits by climate zone. Effectiveness minimums: 50% sensible for most climates. LEED EAc1 credits ERV as a HVAC efficiency measure.

DOAS + ERV System

Dedicated Outdoor Air System with ERV decouples ventilation from space conditioning. Allows downsizing of space conditioning equipment by 20-30%. Enables independent humidity control. When combined with radiant panels or fan coils, DOAS+ERV is considered best-in-class for IAQ and energy performance.

Latent Load Recovery

In hot-humid climates (Zones 1-3), latent cooling can be 30-50% of total cooling load. ERV reducing latent load by 70% can cut total cooling EUI by 15-25%. Rotary wheel or membrane ERVs required for significant latent recovery. MERV-13 pre-filter required to protect ERV media in healthcare applications.

BIM / MEP Coordination

Model DOAS+ERV as a dedicated AHU in Revit MEP with parameters: ERV_Sensible_Effectiveness, ERV_Latent_Effectiveness, ERV_Design_CFM, ERV_Annual_kBtu_Saved. Pressure drop across ERV core (typically 0.5-1.0 in. WG per side) must be included in fan sizing. For LEED EAc1, ERV effectiveness values are required inputs to the energy model — coordinate with energy modeler. Route supply and exhaust ducts to minimize cross-contamination risk; minimum 10 ft separation per ASHRAE 170 for healthcare.
